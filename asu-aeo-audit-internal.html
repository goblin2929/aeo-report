01  /  Executive Summary

AI Knows ASU. It Does Not Reach For ASU.

AI assistants already know Arizona State University. Ask any of them about ASU by name and the answer is accurate, favourable and cites asu.edu directly. We tested that across Google AI Overview, ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity and ASU was cited in every single branded question.

The problem appears the moment a student stops naming the university. When someone describes what they actually want, "a bachelor's degree I can finish online while working full time", or "the strongest nursing programmes", ASU is cited in only 2 of 7 of those conversations. Across all thirty questions we tested, just 8.4 percent of everything the assistants cited belonged to ASU. Reddit was quoted more often than asu.edu, and the University of Arizona was quoted more often than ASU Online.

The site is the reason. ASU has more degree pages than any university we compared, yet the degree site hands Google an unlimited supply of duplicate copies of itself, the highest value commercial pages carry none of the code that tells Google and AI what they offer, and three pages that still bring 13,981 visits a month now open on an error. None of this is a content problem. It is a plumbing problem sitting underneath a genuinely strong estate.

04  /  Site Infrastructure

Three Plumbing Faults Are Doing Most of the Damage

Three things in the plumbing are doing most of the damage. Each is a build task, not a content task.

Critical
The degree site invents pages that do not exist

We asked the degree site for an address that has never existed. Instead of an error it returned the normal degree homepage, and told Google to save it as a real page. A capital letter is enough to trigger it: degrees.asu.edu/Undergraduate and degrees.asu.edu/ are stored as two different pages with the same title.

Why it costs money: Google collects an unlimited number of copies of the degree homepage, splits the strength of the real page between them, and spends its crawling time on copies instead of on real degrees. This is also the reason so many degree pages share one title.

Critical
Pages that still bring visitors now open on an error

Three addresses still rank in Google and still receive visitors, but now return an error page. The largest, the online bachelors page, still brings 4,530 visits a month and holds 609 search terms.

Why it costs money: 13,981 visits a month click a Google result and land on a broken page. The error page then tells Google to save it and claims it is a copy of the ASU Online homepage, so the problem spreads instead of clearing.

High
The most valuable pages do not describe themselves

There is a piece of hidden code that tells Google and AI assistants what a page offers. ASU has it, and it is well built, on degree detail pages. It is missing entirely from ASU Online programme pages, from news articles, and from the explainer library.

07  /  Roadmap and Impact

Roadmap: From 60 to 77

Three horizons. The first two are fixes to what already exists. Only the third asks for something new to be built.

Horizon 1 · 0 to 60 days

Stop the leaks

  • Make the degree site return a real error page, and stop it inventing copies of itself
  • Redirect the three pages that still bring 13,981 visits a month
  • Stop the error page telling Google to save it
  • Make the homepage degree link one permanent jump, and drop the print tracking it adds
  • Name the site map in robots.txt, and fix the file that fails on my.asu.edu
Horizon 2 · 60 to 120 days

Make the money pages readable

  • Copy the degree page code onto every ASU Online programme page
  • Add article code to news and to the explainer library
  • Write descriptions for the homepage and every page above 3,000 visits a month
  • Give each degree search page a title built from what the student searched
  • Point ASU's identity code at the right address and add Wikipedia and Wikidata
Horizon 3 · 120 days and beyond

Build the engine

  • Pick two or three topics ASU is genuinely expert in with a degree behind each
  • Build 15 to 30 linked pages per topic rather than one page
  • Start with askabiologist, which already earns 91,363 visits and needs depth plus a route to the degrees
  • Create the internal linking map that points every topic page at its matching degree
  • Publish llms.txt while none of the five competitors has one
