Executive Summary

Period in Review: the story behind the numbers

Clicks eased to 77,129 (-7.80%) and impressions to 22,965,144 (-11.51%), but this is not a ranking decline. Average position actually improved from 7.0 to 6.8 and CTR rose +4.19%, and branded clicks grew +14.21%. The pattern points to softer search demand on a few very large page groups, not lost rankings. Reading each segment:

  • Currency converter drives the impression drop, and it is demand-side, not ranking. The converter pages hold hundreds of thousands of impressions each and naturally swing with the FX news cycle. Example: the query "dolar ke rupiah" fell from 210,461 to 88,277 impressions, yet its position held at 9.82 (from 10.13). When impressions fall but position holds, that is trending, not a ranking loss. The action is to lift these pages from position 7 to 10 into the top 5 so the still-large pools convert (see Target Keyword Performance).
  • Blog is the stable base and held up. Blog carries 59,272 clicks (77% of the site) with impressions down only -1.22% and position steady (7.4 to 7.3). Clicks dipped -5.79% on a mild CTR softening, so the lever here is title and meta refreshes on the top decliners, not new visibility.
  • Globe is the one real ranking signal to watch. Unlike the rest of the site, Globe's average position worsened (9.1 to 9.9). The decline is concentrated in a few pages (Philippines, Japan, Singapore) and in the English Globe cluster that ranks at positions 13 to 40. The priority Indonesian pages (Malaysia, China, Australia) actually grew. See the Flip Globe deep dive.
  • Branded demand is healthy (+14.21% clicks). The dip is category and seasonal, not a brand problem.

What Moved in Globe, and why

  • The decline is not uniform, it is a handful of pages. Three pages account for most of the drop: Philippines (-56.25%, 32 to 14 clicks), Japan (-40.00%, 25 to 15), and Singapore (-38.46%, 13 to 8). Everything else was flat or grew.
  • Priority Indonesian pages are mostly winning. Malaysia grew +6.12% and its position jumped from 6.38 to 4.91; China grew +128.57% (position 13.40 to 9.64); Australia grew +166.67% (position 6.29 to 4.90). The Indonesian-language Globe pages are trending up.
  • Philippines is a query-mix story, not a ranking loss. The Indonesian page /id/globe/philippines actually improved position (5.10 to 4.48) yet lost clicks, which means it lost a few specific high-CTR queries rather than dropping in rank. The English /en/globe/philippines is the real slip (position 7.35 to 10.01).
  • The English Globe cluster drags the average. English Globe pages rank at positions 13 to 40 (Australia 19.34, Singapore 16.35, USA 23.95, UAE 37.42). This is what pulls Globe's overall position from 9.1 to 9.9, and it is exactly what the planned dynamic-content build should fix.
