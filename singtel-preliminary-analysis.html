Executive Summary

AI already knows Singtel. Across 50 buyer questions run through Google AI Overview, ChatGPT and Gemini, the brand was named in 82% of answers. The problem is the other side of the funnel: the site is not built to capitalise on that awareness, and at the discovery stage AI cites Circles.Life, comparison sites and global eSIM players instead of Singtel.

The gap in one line. LLM Visibility 57 versus Site Readiness 47. AI knows the brand, but weak schema, client side rendering that hides product pages from crawlers, a homepage with no H1 and critical mobile performance stop that awareness from turning into citations and clicks.

Two numbers frame the commercial cost. First, Singtel is cited by AI in 60% of all prompts but only 57% of non branded ones, and it wins decision and branded questions (BOFU 89%, Branded 75%) while losing awareness (TOFU 33%). Second, Ahrefs shows Singtel is 79% dependent on branded search, and earns non branded product traffic on only 3 pages versus M1 on 108 pages. The same root cause sits under both: people who already know Singtel find it; people discovering the category do not.

This analysis validates the 9 findings supplied by the Singtel side and adds 5 from NovaStacks. Every finding below was checked against the live server HTML before it was written.

Findings

14 findings. Technical and content items were confirmed by NovaStacks against the live site; the 5 marked NovaStacks are new.

#AreaFindingValidated observation and why it mattersSuggestion
1 Technical No structured data on the homepage, and invalid product schema
singtel.com (homepage), /personal/products-services/shop/fibre
 Validated. The homepage source contains zero JSON-LD (no Organization or WebSite entity markup). On the fibre shop page the Product and Offer objects are present but every Offer omits availability, url and priceValidUntil, and the Products omit review or aggregateRating, so Rich Results Test reports the Product markup as invalid. Without a clean entity block on the homepage, Google and AI engines cannot reliably resolve Singtel as an entity (logo, sameAs, sitelinks search box), and the invalid product markup forfeits Product rich results on the flagship commercial page. Add valid Organization and WebSite JSON-LD to the homepage. Fix the Offer objects on product pages by adding availability, url and priceValidUntil, and add review or aggregateRating where genuine.
2 Technical No structured data on the blog article
/personal/singtel-blog/right-mobile-plan
 Validated. The article source contains only BreadcrumbList and ListItem JSON-LD. There is no Article or BlogPosting and no FAQPage. Article and FAQPage schema make a post eligible for rich results and give LLMs machine readable context (author, publish date, topic, question and answer). This is a high intent commercial topic (choosing a mobile plan), so the lost visibility maps to lost plan sign ups. Add Article (or BlogPosting) and, where the content answers questions, FAQPage JSON-LD to blog templates.
3 Technical Favicon missing on product pages
/personal/products-services/shop/fibre
 Validated. The fibre shop page source has no favicon link at all, while other templates reference an SVG favicon (the homepage and wifi6 use an absolute URL, the blog uses a relative path). The favicon shows next to the URL on mobile results, in AI assistant panels and in browser tabs, so a missing favicon on the highest commercial intent pages reduces brand recognition at the click and signals technical inconsistency. Add a consistent favicon link to every template, using an absolute URL so it resolves on all paths.
4 Technical Product links are not crawlable (client side rendering)
/personal/products-services/shop/fibre, /personal/products-services/broadband/wifi6
 Validated and more severe than first flagged. The fibre page is a Next.js client rendered app; its server HTML exposes only asset file links and no product links, so the wifi6 URL cannot be discovered from it. The homepage similarly exposes only 9 anchor links in server HTML. A page reachable only through JavaScript receives almost no internal link equity, may never be indexed and is invisible to LLMs. Ahrefs confirms the cost: Singtel earns non branded product traffic on only 3 pages (5,304 visits) versus M1 on 108 pages (15,432 visits). Render primary navigation and product links as real anchor elements in server HTML (server side rendering or static links), not JavaScript only click handlers.
5 Technical Product sub pages have no canonical
/personal/products-services/broadband/wifi6
 Validated. The wifi6 page has no canonical tag in source. Without a self referencing canonical, Google picks its own, which lets parameter or duplicate versions compete and can index the wrong URL, splitting ranking signals. Add a self referencing canonical to every product sub page.
6 Technical Blog articles have no canonical
/personal/singtel-blog/right-mobile-plan
 Validated. The article has no canonical tag in source. Blog URLs frequently collect UTM and campaign parameters when shared, so a missing canonical lets those variants dilute the article's ranking signals and can index the wrong URL. Add a self referencing canonical to every blog article template.
7 Technical External domain (gomo.sg) inside the Singtel sitemap [confirm intent]
/sitemap-explore.xml
 Validated. The sitemap at singtel.com/sitemap-explore.xml contains 88 URLs pointing to www.gomo.sg. A sitemap is a crawl instruction scoped to the host it is submitted for; a different domain inside it is treated as a cross submission that Google ignores, and it can trigger sitemap warnings in Search Console while muddying crawl signals for the real Singtel URLs. If GOMO must be promoted, it should be handled through its own sitemap. Confirm whether the GOMO inclusion is intentional. Best practice is to remove the external domain and keep Singtel's sitemap to singtel.com URLs only.
8 Technical No clear path from the homepage to the blog
singtel.com (homepage)
 Validated. The homepage source contains zero links to /personal/singtel-blog/. If the content hub has no link from the homepage or main navigation, crawlers reach it slowly and pass little authority to it, and users and AI assistants struggle to find it. This caps how much non branded awareness and consideration traffic the blog can capture. Add a clear, crawlable link from the homepage and main navigation to the blog hub and its key articles.
9 Content The blog is not actively updated
/personal/singtel-blog/
 Validated against live AI behaviour. Telecom demand shifts constantly (plans, roaming, 5G, eSIM, seasonal deals). In our AI visibility run, AI overwhelmingly cites editorial and comparison content: 762 Article or Blog citations, 122 Comparison or Review, 56 Forum, out of about 1,050. Singtel's blog is cited in only a few awareness prompts while aggregators and Circles.Life win discovery. Stale content loses freshness signals and is rarely cited by AI. Run an always on content cadence targeting current non branded problems (best SIM only, roaming vs travel eSIM, fibre comparisons, coverage) and refresh top articles on a schedule.
10 NovaStacks Homepage has no H1
singtel.com (homepage)
 New finding, validated. The homepage source contains zero H1 elements (it has 10 H2s). The most important page therefore has no primary heading, weakening the top level topical signal and the heading hierarchy that both search and AI use to build a page outline. Add a single descriptive H1 to the homepage that states who Singtel is and the core services.
11 NovaStacks Root homepage canonical points to /personal [confirm intent]
singtel.com (homepage)
 New finding, validated. The page at singtel.com/ declares its canonical as https://www.singtel.com/personal. This tells Google the canonical version of the root is /personal. If /personal is deliberately the primary landing page this may be intended, but if the root should rank as the homepage it is ceding that signal. Confirm the intended canonical for the root. If the root is the homepage, self reference it.
12 NovaStacks Weak semantic HTML across templates
singtel.com, /personal/products-services/broadband/wifi6, /personal/singtel-blog/right-mobile-plan
 New finding, validated. The homepage uses no main, article, section, header, footer or aside landmarks; the product and blog templates expose only a nav element. Semantic landmarks help AI separate primary content from navigation and boilerplate, so div heavy markup lowers extraction quality and citability. Introduce semantic landmarks (main, article, section, header, footer) and one clean H1 to H3 hierarchy per template.
13 NovaStacks Product sub pages carry no Product schema
/personal/products-services/broadband/wifi6
 New finding, validated. The wifi6 product page carries only BreadcrumbList; it has no Product, Offer or FAQ schema. The rich Product markup exists on the main shop page but not on the broader product template set, so eligible product rich results are absent across many product pages. Extend the Product and FAQ schema pattern from the shop page to the full product page template, with valid Offer fields.
14 NovaStacks Critical mobile performance on the homepage
singtel.com (homepage)
 New finding, validated (lab). DataForSEO Lighthouse mobile scored the homepage at performance 0.01 with LCP 53.7s, CLS 1.0 and TBT 3.83s, well below M1 (0.26) and GOMO (0.38). This is a single lab run on an emulated mobile device, so it should be confirmed against field data, but it aligns with the heavy client side rendering seen above. Slow pages are crawled less often and rank worse. Reduce main thread work and payload on the homepage (defer non critical JavaScript, stabilise layout to fix CLS, prioritise the hero for LCP). Confirm with CrUX field data.

Opportunities & Next Actions

Both estimates below are realistic, not ceilings. They already account for the two forces that take clicks away from organic listings in Singapore today: AI Overviews, which answer some queries directly without a click, and paid search (SEM), where competitors bid on the same terms. In other words these are the clicks we expect to earn after those effects, not the raw search volume.

Opportunity 1 · Recover non-branded product traffic

Realistic estimate: about 10,000 visits per month (roughly 120,000 per year). This estimate is the observed gap to M1, who already competes in the same search results with AI Overviews and paid ads present. Because it is measured from real captured traffic, it is already net of those effects.

Insight. Singtel earns non-branded product traffic on only 3 pages; M1 does it on 108, which is 15,432 versus 5,304 visits per month. The cause is not missing pages, it is how they are built. Singtel's product and device pages are client-side rendered, so a crawler sees a near-empty shell. We confirmed this live: the Oppo A6 Pro page on shop.singtel.com serves a $0 placeholder and no specifications in the server HTML, while M1's page for the same phone serves 17 real prices and full specs. That one difference is why the same phone ranks #11 for Singtel and #1 for M1, and why the Samsung S25 FE page exists but does not rank at all.

Next actions.

  • Improve the content inside the product pages (PDPs): server-side render the specifications, price and plan detail on product and device pages, and rewrite titles to the "[Product] Specs, Price, Singapore" pattern that M1 uses.
  • Create dedicated eSIM and roaming landing pages. These are missing today, and the eSIM head term (8,200 per month) sits at position #30.
  • Add pages for genuinely missing device models (for example the Vivo V60 page currently returns a 404).

Opportunity 2 · Expand the blog to fill the content topic gap

Realistic estimate: about 15,000 visits per month (roughly 180,000 per year). The ceiling for these ten topics is about 50,000 visits per month if Singtel ranked first on every one. The realistic figure is roughly 30 percent of that, because Singtel will not rank first everywhere, and AI Overviews and paid ads absorb part of the clicks.

Insight. Singtel's blog is mostly appliance buying guides (washing machines, vacuums, headphones, tablets). The telco educational topics that search and AI actually reward, such as WiFi troubleshooting, roaming, eSIM and broadband speed, are owned by MoneySmart, M1's own blog and global players like Airalo. This matters for AI visibility too: in our run, AI cited editorial and how-to content in 762 of about 1,050 citations, the exact format the current blog does not produce.

Next actions.

  • Publish and maintain a telco how-to content track covering the topics below, then refresh them on a schedule.
  • Prioritise the two fastest, lowest-competition wins first: WiFi troubleshooting (Singtel should own "singtel wifi down") and the eSIM travel guides (low difficulty, currently handed to Airalo and Holafly).
