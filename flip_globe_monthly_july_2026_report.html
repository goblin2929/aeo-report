Executive Summary

Period in Review: the parts we optimise grew; the converter softened on demand

The headline Globe-scope clicks fell -25.43%, but that is entirely the currency converter (the largest area) softening on demand. The two areas we are actively working, Flip Globe and the globe-related blogs, both grew, and the heading implementation is showing in the priority Globe pages. Reading each area:

  • Flip Globe grew +14.13% (1,168 to 1,333 clicks) with its average position improving from 8.67 to 8.47. The priority pages moved up: China improved from position 11.41 to 8.30 (clicks +31.82%), Philippines +78.26% (position 4.99 to 5.27 stable), English Malaysia +26.32% (position 10.92 to 9.90). This is the destination heading work landing at the monthly level.
  • Globe-related blogs grew +23.04% (6,131 to 7,544 clicks) with positions improving, led by the SWIFT-code cluster (swift-code-bri improved 9.41 to 7.86, +79% clicks).
  • Currency Converter Indonesian fell -27.53% on demand, not ranking. Impressions dropped -21.14% (June had unusually high currency-search interest) while the average position actually improved from 6.98 to 6.83. When clicks and impressions fall together but the position holds or improves, it is a seasonal or demand swing, not a ranking loss. The USD page still holds about 1.5 million impressions at position 8.4, the standing opportunity.
  • Currency English eased -9.21% but impressions grew +11.36% and the usd-usa-idr page improved (position 9.20 to 8.83). English remains the biggest untapped ceiling ("usd to idr", 1.83M searches).
  • Net: the engagement's controllable levers (Globe pages and blogs) are trending up; the converter number is a demand cycle to ride out while we push the big pools into the click zone.

4. Technical hygiene: broken converter hreflang between the ID and EN versions (reminder)

Carried forward. The Indonesian and English converters use different path slugs (/id/kalkulator-mata-uang/ vs /en/currency-converter/), but the hreflang alternate tags only swap the /id/ and /en/ prefix and keep the wrong path, so each language points to a broken URL for the other. The ID page en-US alternate points to a 404, and the EN page id-ID and x-default point to a 301 redirect.

Current, ID page head (broken)
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="en-US" href="https://flip.id/en/kalkulator-mata-uang/idr-mys-myr"/> <-- 404 (wrong path)
Fixed, ID page head
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="en-US" href="https://flip.id/en/currency-converter/idr-mys-myr"/>

The rule: the alternate href must map the language prefix AND the path slug together (id + kalkulator-mata-uang, en + currency-converter), and always point to the final 200 URL. Apply across every currency-pair page.
