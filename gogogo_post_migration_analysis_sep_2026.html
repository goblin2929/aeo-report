Data source: Google Search Console (aeo-novastacks profile) | Window: 14 to 26 September 2026 | Prepared by Novastacks | Client: Gogogo (Flip Group)

How this is measured

On 21 September the Indonesian site moved from gogogo.id to gogogo.com/id-id. During a migration Google keeps the old URLs in search for a while, then swaps to the new pages one by one as it reindexes, so traffic splits across both. We read the true Indonesian condition by combining gogogo.id (old) plus gogogo.com/id-id (new).

Scope check: the new side is strictly the /id-id locale only, never /en-my, /th-th or the domain root. We verified this directly: every gogogo.com click counted starts with gogogo.com/id-id, with zero leakage from other locales. The /id-id figure correctly includes the /id-id homepage. Latest fully settled day is 26 September.

2. PDP condition: the three champion pages and their top five non-branded keywords

The new URLs are indexed and starting to rank, but the head keywords are entering at much weaker positions than the old URLs

This is the real signal to watch. Combined clicks held (section 1) mainly because the old URLs were still ranking. As Google swaps to the new gogogo.com/id-id pages (already happening for Free Fire and Mobile Legends around 25 to 26 September), the new pages are currently ranking well below where the old pages sat for the money keywords. They are still being reindexed on the new domain and their positions are still settling, which takes time after a domain move, and this needs to progress for traffic to hold once the old URLs fully drop out.

So, is it recovering?

Early signs yes, but incomplete. The new pages are indexed and appearing, and Roblox is nearly at parity. Free Fire and Mobile Legends are still ranking well below the old URLs on their head keywords, so their recovery has only just started. The single most important thing to watch over the next four weeks is the new gogogo.com/id-id URLs completing reindexing, since moving from the old domain to the new one takes time for the head keyword positions to settle.

4. Technical signals: redirects, response codes and indexing

The redirect setup is correct: gogogo.id points one to one at gogogo.com/id-id

The redirects were put in place at the 21 September migration and remain correctly configured. We verified them again live on 29 September: every gogogo.id URL returns an HTTP 308 permanent redirect to the exact matching gogogo.com/id-id URL, with the path preserved, including the three champion PDPs.

gogogo.id/p/topup/mobile-legends-games308 permanentgogogo.com/id-id/p/topup/mobile-legends-games
gogogo.id/p/topup/voucher-roblox-robux-games308 permanentgogogo.com/id-id/p/topup/voucher-roblox-robux-games
gogogo.id/blog/308 permanentgogogo.com/id-id/blog/

A one to one permanent redirect is the correct way to migrate: Google consolidates the old page signals onto the new URL. This is exactly what we want to see, and it explains the response code pattern below.

Response codes and indexing (Search Console)

  • gogogo.id 200 responses dropping toward zero: expected and healthy. Because gogogo.id now redirects (308) instead of serving pages, Search Console records fewer 200 responses on the old domain. The Crawl Stats chart below shows OK (200) crawl requests falling to near zero in the last days of September, which confirms the redirects are live.
  • gogogo.com 200 responses increasing: expected and healthy. The Crawl Stats chart below shows OK (200) crawl requests on the new domain climbing, peaking at 5,879 on 21 September (migration day), a positive sign that Google is picking up the migration.
  • Page Indexing report not yet updated: Search Console has not generated an updated Page Indexing report for the new state since 21 September. This is normal this soon after a migration; we are waiting for Google to process and populate it, and will review it once available.

What to watch closely and action items

This is the migration transition we need to watch closely over the next four weeks

Combined clicks are holding, but the new gogogo.com/id-id URLs are still ranking below the old ones on the head keywords, so the migration is not yet fully settled. We are watching these signals weekly:

  • The new gogogo.com/id-id URLs for "top up ff" and "top up ml" completing reindexing and their positions settling, since a move from the old domain (.id) to the new domain (.com) takes time.
  • The new blog volume ramping as the old blog URLs hand over, and the new blog position holding.
  • The combined daily Indonesian clicks holding as gogogo.id fully swaps to gogogo.com/id-id.

The specific action items to support the migration and recovery are tracked here: Post Migration Action Items.

Recommendation: periodic press releases to accelerate indexation and build trust

To speed up how quickly Google and the AI platforms pick up the move, and to build user trust and credibility for the new domain, we recommend Gogogo publish periodic press releases that clearly announce the migration. A public, dated announcement gives search engines and AI answer engines an authoritative signal that the brand has moved, and reassures users that gogogo.com is the official domain.

  • Indonesia: announce that Gogogo is moving from gogogo.id to gogogo.com/id-id.
  • Philippines: announce that Gogogo is moving from gogogo.game/en-ph to gogogo.com/en-ph.

Summary

  • No click loss yet: combined Indonesian clicks are +15.3%, because the old URLs still ranked through most of the window.
  • But the leading indicator is weaker: the new gogogo.com/id-id URLs are entering at much lower positions for the head keywords (Free Fire and Mobile Legends "top up" terms at page two to six versus page one on the old URLs). Roblox has transferred best and is near parity.
  • The blog is mid handover with a visible dip (-17.5% combined) as old blog URLs drop out and the new blog ramps; the new blog position is close to the old, which is a good sign.
  • What to watch (next four weeks): the new gogogo.com/id-id URLs for "top up ff" and "top up ml" still being reindexed and their positions settling, the new blog volume ramping, and the combined daily figure holding. Moving a site from the old domain (.id) to the new domain (.com) takes time for Google to fully reindex, so this is the normal migration transition and is not yet complete.
