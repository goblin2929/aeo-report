September 2026 Strategy & Priorities (incl. AEO Focus Areas)

  1. Revisit sub-headings and FAQs on the product pages. Now that the August launch has generated a fresh body of tool-related queries, rewrite the sub-headings and FAQ set against what people actually asked. Working doc: product page sub-heading and FAQ doc ContentAEO
  2. Improve the existing YouTube descriptions. Fuller context in every description plus internal links into the matching product pages, so the video library feeds the site rather than sitting beside it ContentAEO
  3. Backlink profile clean-up. The hypothesis is that some links from past activity point at Decube with irrelevant anchor text, and that some referring domains have an irrelevant history. Needs Decube's team: the list of domains that redirect into decube.io, so we can separate Decube's own domains from the spam operator's before anything is disavowed Technical
  4. Rebuild and re-optimise the comparison pages. Both the high intent head-to-head pages and a universal "Decube vs every tool" page built as the single source of truth, formatted so AI platforms can lift it directly ContentAEO
  5. Build competitor versus competitor pages. Pages that answer "Atlan vs Alation", "Atlan vs Collibra", "Alation vs Collibra" and the rest on their merits, with Decube's features and advantages positioned as a third option inside the comparison rather than bolted on the end. Why: Decube has far less top of mind than the leaders in this category, so waiting to be searched by name is a losing strategy. Buyers compare the vendors they already know, and that is the moment to be present. Evidence is in the section below: Decube already surfaces for 48 competitor comparison queries and 861 impressions in August without one page written for them ContentAEO
  6. 301 the important 404 pages. Recover the equity sitting on dead URLs by redirecting each to its closest live equivalent instead of leaving it to return a 404. The pattern is already established on the site (/data-catalog redirects to /metadata-management, /copilot to /trusty). What we can already see: of the 1,111 URLs Search Console reported impressions for in July or August, we live-checked all 37 real page URLs absent from the pages registry and found two dead URLs, both at 1 impression and 0 clicks, so nothing is bleeding traffic today. What we cannot see, and what we need: the Search Analytics API only returns URLs that still earn impressions, so a 404 that has already lost its rankings is invisible to it, and those are usually the ones worth redirecting. The full list sits in the Page Indexing report under "Not found (404)", which is a UI export. We will take that export, cross it against broken backlinks, and 301 every URL holding links or historic clicks. Prioritised by links and past traffic, not by 404 count Technical
Items to monitor: ChatGPT returned on 31 August after a 41 day absence but is still only 0.06% for the month against 4.90% on AI Overview, so judge the September work on whether that second engine stays open · data governance slipped 26.1 to 37.1 average position while data catalog platforms gained 50 places, so the head term needs a dedicated look · the adult and piracy query attack was still running through the end of August, and every number in this report excludes it, and the disavow file remains the open action · the 404 redirect sweep cannot be scoped until we have the Page Indexing "Not found" export, because the Search Analytics API cannot see a URL that has already lost all of its impressions.

The case for competitor versus competitor pages, in the data:
  • Decube already ranks for the comparison, just not deliberately. The top non-brand query on /atlan-vs-decube is "atlan vs alation" (33 impressions, position 24.8), then "atlan vs datahub" (17 impressions, position 18.8). Google is already trying to serve a Decube page for these searches and has to use a page written about something else. A page built for the query starts from a far better place than position 24.
  • The top of mind gap is measurable, and it is the whole argument. Atlan holds 41.43% AI visibility and Alation 33.98% against Decube's 2.45%. Buyers do not search for a vendor they have not heard of, but they do compare the two they have. Being the third name inside that comparison is the cheapest way into a consideration set Decube is not currently in.
  • The best positions are on natural language buying questions, which is exactly what AI assistants and AI Overview fan out into: "atlan vs collibra vs informatica for enterprise data governance, as a financial services company which one to choose for regulatory compliance?" sits at position 6.8, "are there better alternatives to atlan that cost less than more established players like collibra with better roi?" at 5.3, and "atlan vs. collibra, which is a better data governance tool?" at 1.0. This is an AEO play first and an SEO play second.
  • The format is already proven on this domain. The three biggest AI citation gains in August were all comparison and alternatives pages: best-data-governance-platforms-for-banks (116 citations from zero), open-source-data-catalog-comparison (29) and alation-alternatives (13). AI engines quote this format, and Decube has very few pages in it.
One caution to build in: a page comparing two rivals has to be genuinely useful about those two rivals, or it earns neither links nor citations and reads as bait. Lead with an honest comparison, keep Decube as a clearly marked third option, and keep every competitor claim sourced and dated so the page stays defensible as their products change.

Opening it properly is a different job from AI Overview (third-party citation sources rather than owned pages) and it belongs on the September list alongside the comparison page rebuild.

Methodology & Validation

Adult and piracy query exclusion. From 22 August a spam network pointed adult and piracy anchor text at 22 Decube pages, and Google Indonesia began returning the Decube homepage for those queries. In raw Search Console data this shows as 2,918 August clicks against 2,161 in July, which would read as growth. It is not. Every figure in this report removes it, in two layers. Layer one: a 112 term regex covering the bokep family and its misspellings, explicit adult vocabulary in English and Bahasa Indonesia, adult streaming and piracy hosts, and the strings Search Console masks with asterisks. The pattern was built from the complete 25,576 row August query export and audited in both directions, so every match was reviewed for false positives and every non-match reviewed for leakage. It removes 1,194 of August's clicks and 99 of July's, and a page-dimension check confirms 100% of them landed on the homepage. Layer two: Indonesia and India are excluded from every period in the charts and the segment tables. Search Console anonymises rare queries, so a long tail of adult clicks is never labelled and the regex cannot see it; Indonesian clicks went from a 53 to 125 per month baseline to 1,473 in August, and Indian clicks were inflated to 1,182 in June and 959 in July by the same network's South Asian arm. Excluding both markets uniformly across all eight months is the only way to compare months honestly. For reference, on the adult-regex-only basis August totals 1,724 clicks against July's 2,062.

Dimension discipline. Site totals and the charts use the date dimension, which never double counts. Page and query breakdowns use their own dimensions and are labelled as such. Page-dimension impressions are counted once per page shown and so run above the date-dimension totals; page-dimension clicks cross-validate at 1.007 against date-dimension clicks for August and 1.003 for July. A caution recorded for future reports: combining a country filter with the page dimension makes Search Console suppress roughly 44% of clicks, so all page-level tables here are pulled unfiltered and corrected by subtraction instead.

AEO measurement. Visibility is the share of tracked AI responses in which Decube is mentioned, averaged across the days in the month. A response is one engine asked one prompt on one day. WorkDuo re-asks the panel daily; August carries 31 days of data and July carries 27 (tracking started 5 July). The prompt panel is identical across both months, so no part of the gain comes from adding prompts. Google AI Overview and ChatGPT are read with the singular platform= filter, verified against the cross-platform aggregate so the filter is known to apply.

AI sessions come from GA4 directly, not from a dashboard estimate: sessions whose sessionSource matches the AI platform list (chatgpt, openai, perplexity, gemini, bard, claude, anthropic, copilot, you.com, poe, deepseek, grok, mistral, phind, searchgpt, edgeservices, aimode). The same definition is used for every client and every month.
