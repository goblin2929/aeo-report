// 01 · Executive Summary

Winning the AI answer, not yet the foundation beneath it

Rently is a rent flexibility platform operating one brand across two markets: rently.sg in Singapore (pay, defer, and earn rewards on rent) and rently-uae.com in the UAE (Rent Now, Pay Later, annual rent split into monthly instalments). We tested both as a real renter would, and the headline is the same in each: AI already recommends Rently.

Across 15 real buyer questions per market, Rently was cited in 100 percent of them by Google AI Overview, Gemini, and ChatGPT. In the UAE it is cited far more often than Keyper, the organic market leader, even though Keyper has roughly ten times its search traffic. The catch is that this visibility is carried almost entirely by blog articles. The homepage and product pages, the pages that convert, are rarely the ones cited, and underneath them the technical foundation (structured data, headings, breadcrumbs, sitemap coverage, page speed) is incomplete. Rently is excellent at being recommended, and weak at capturing that recommendation on the pages that sell.

Rently does not need to win AI visibility, it already has it. The opportunity is to protect it and point it at the pages that convert, before competitors (CardUp in Singapore, Keyper in the UAE) close the gap. Three moves do this, and each is winnable demand rather than vanity volume.

How to read this

The UAE calculator is the one clean traffic number: a RERA rent increase calculator targets roughly 5,000 low difficulty searches a month, modelled at about plus 550 organic visits a month once it ranks. The bigger prize is structural: Rently is already cited by AI in both markets, so the work is not to earn attention but to make the homepage and product pages as citable as the blog already is, and to stop wasting the ~80 percent of Singapore blog pages and the dormant UAE blog that currently earn nothing. Figures are directional because Search Console is not yet connected, and tighten once it is.

This analysis groups the findings into three areas: Technical, Content, and Future Development. Each finding explains what we found, why it matters for a non technical reader, and what we recommend. The two market sections that follow show where each site stands today.

// 04 · Technical Findings

How the sites speak to search engines and AI

Findings validated against the live sites. Each is tagged with the market it applies to. Structured data was checked in Rich Results Test, the way the client checks it.

4.1  Structured data produces no rich results
SingaporeTechnicalHigh Impact
What we found

In Google Rich Results Test, rently.sg returns no rich results, on the homepage, product pages, and blog. The page source does contain JSON-LD, but only non rendering entity types (WebSite, WebPage, FinancialServices) that Rich Results Test does not surface. The rich result types that matter, FAQPage, BreadcrumbList, Article, and Product, are absent from JSON-LD. The FAQ that shows on the page exists only as microdata, not JSON-LD, and one blog JSON-LD block is invalid.

Why it matters

Structured data is how a site explains itself to machines. Without the rich result types, Google shows no enhanced result and AI engines get minimal machine readable signal about what Rently is and does. This is a direct reason the product pages are rarely the ones AI cites.

Recommendation

Add real JSON-LD for the rich types: FAQPage (convert the existing microdata), BreadcrumbList, Article or BlogPosting on the blog, and Product or Service on the product pages. Fix the invalid blog block.

4.2  The main heading is hidden from users
SingaporeTechnicalHigh Impact
What we found

The homepage Heading 1 ("The Flexible Way to Pay Rent in Singapore.") and most section headings are marked sr-only, meaning they are hidden from sighted users and only read by screen readers and crawlers. So the heading Google and AI read is not the visible hero text a user sees. (This was initially reported as an old design cache; the live source is actually current, the real cause is the hidden heading.)

Why it matters

The Heading 1 is the single most important on page signal of what a page is about. When the real heading is hidden and does not match the visible page, the strongest keyword signal on the most important page is muddled.

Recommendation

Make the primary heading a visible, keyword aligned Heading 1, and let the visible hero text be that heading rather than a separate hidden one.

4.3  Structured data is inconsistent across templates
UAETechnicalHigh Impact
What we found

In Rich Results Test, rently-uae.com is inconsistent rather than simply empty. The homepage returns no rich results (zero valid JSON-LD, only Question and Answer microdata) and the blog JSON-LD is broken and invalid. But the product pages actually pass, with valid BreadcrumbList, FAQPage, and HowTo (plus Service and Organization). The rich schema pattern already exists, it just is not applied everywhere.

Why it matters

The most important page, the homepage, and the biggest content asset, the blog, are the two that machines cannot read, while the product pages that could be reused as the template are the ones done well. This is fixable by standardising what already exists.

Recommendation

Standardise the product page JSON-LD across all templates. Add Organization and WebSite sitewide, fix the homepage (currently zero schema) and the broken blog JSON-LD, and add Article or BlogPosting to blog posts.

4.4  Blog posts are missing from the XML sitemap
UAETechnicalHigh Impact
What we found

The sitemap contains 90 URLs, but only 1 of them is a blog URL (the index). The roughly 22 published blog posts are absent from the sitemap.

Why it matters

The sitemap is the primary list Google uses to discover and prioritise crawling. Blog posts left out get slower, incomplete, or no indexing, so content that was paid for never gets a chance to rank. This is the single biggest reason the UAE blog earns almost nothing today.

Recommendation

Add every blog post to the XML sitemap and submit it in Search Console. This is the fastest win in the UAE.

4.5  No breadcrumb navigation on blog pages
SingaporeUAETechnicalMedium Impact
What we found

Blog pages on both sites have no breadcrumb navigation and no BreadcrumbList structured data. (On the UAE site, the product pages do have BreadcrumbList, so the pattern exists, it just is not applied to the blog.)

Why it matters

Breadcrumbs show users and crawlers where a page sits in the site. Without them, blog pages look like orphaned, standalone URLs, which weakens internal link equity and how the listing appears in search results.

Recommendation

Add breadcrumbs with structure Home > Blog > Category > Article, and the matching BreadcrumbList structured data.

4.6  Poor mobile page speed
SingaporeUAETechnicalMedium Impact
What we found

On Lighthouse mobile, rently.sg scores 0.43 with a Largest Contentful Paint of 22.5 seconds and Time to Interactive of 24.8 seconds. rently-uae.com scores 0.55 with an LCP of 7.5 seconds. Layout stability (CLS) is good on both.

Why it matters

Slow pages are crawled less often, rank lower, and lose conversions at the moment a renter is deciding. The Singapore homepage in particular is far outside the acceptable range.

Recommendation

Optimise hero loading and hydration, target an LCP under 2.5 seconds on both homepages, starting with rently.sg.

4.7  Semantic HTML landmarks missing
SingaporeUAETechnicalMedium Impact
What we found

Neither homepage uses the standard landmark elements. rently.sg has no main, article, or header element and leans on hidden headings and div containers. rently-uae.com has no main, nav, header, or footer, though it does have a real visible Heading 1.

Why it matters

Landmark elements help AI crawlers separate real content from navigation and boilerplate, so clean content extraction (and citation) is easier. Div heavy markup makes that harder.

Recommendation

Introduce main, article or section, header, and footer landmarks with a consistent heading hierarchy.

4.8  Canonical does not match the sitemap
UAETechnicalQuick Win
What we found

The homepage canonical is rently-uae.com (no trailing slash) while the sitemap lists rently-uae.com/ (with a trailing slash).

Why it matters

Small inconsistencies between the canonical and the sitemap can split signals and make Google guess which form is authoritative.

Recommendation

Normalise the canonical and sitemap URLs to one consistent form.

// 05 · Content Findings

How clearly each page explains its product

5.1  Homepage and product titles waste space and miss the keyword
SingaporeContentHigh Impact
What we found

The title template prepends "Rently | " and pages also end with "| Rently", so the brand appears twice in the same title, on the homepage, every product page, and every blog page (for example "Rently | Delay Your Rent... | Rently"). The homepage title carries product descriptors but not the transactional phrase a buyer searches, "pay rent online". Our AI test confirms the cost: for that query AI cites a blog guide, not the homepage.

  • https://www.rently.sg/delay-rent
  • https://www.rently.sg/lower-move-in-costs
Why it matters

The title is the clickable line in search results and a primary ranking signal. Repeating the brand wastes the limited space that should carry the focus keyword, and the money query lands on an article instead of the page that converts.

Recommendation

Remove the duplicate brand and rewrite each title around the product and its benefit, giving the homepage the transactional keyword (for example "Pay Rent Online by Card in Singapore, Earn Rewards and Defer Rent | Rently").

5.2  Around 80 percent of the blog earns no visibility
SingaporeContentHigh Impact
What we found

The sitemap lists 150 blog URLs, but Ahrefs shows only about 31 blog pages with any organic traffic. Roughly 120 pages, about 80 percent of the blog, earn nothing.

Why it matters

That is real content investment returning nothing. It usually means the pages target keywords with no demand, compete with each other, or do not match search intent. The dead weight also dilutes the topical authority of the pages that do work.

Recommendation

Audit the non performing pages, then consolidate, re optimise, or retire them, and redirect the effort toward the clusters that already earn AI citations and traffic.

5.3  Homepage title is a slogan with no keyword
UAEContentHigh Impact
What we found

The homepage title is "Rently | The better way to rent", a slogan that carries no search keyword. The visible Heading 1, "Rent now, pay later.", is good and should inform the title.

Why it matters

The highest authority page on the site is invisible to the high intent queries the product answers, such as "rent now pay later dubai" and "pay rent with credit card uae". Google then ranks a weaker page, or nothing, for the money query.

Recommendation

Rewrite the homepage title around the real product, for example "Rent Now, Pay Later in the UAE, Pay Rent Monthly Instead of Upfront | Rently".

5.4  The blog is dormant
UAEContentHigh Impact
What we found

The UAE blog earns only about 24 organic visits across roughly 22 pages, and (per finding 4.4) those pages are not even in the sitemap. The channel is effectively switched off.

Why it matters

The blog is the main non paid acquisition channel and, as the AI test shows, the very thing carrying Rently's AI visibility. An abandoned blog stops compounding authority and slowly decays, which is dangerous when the blog is what keeps Rently ahead of Keyper in AI answers.

Recommendation

Reactivate the blog against high intent clusters (RERA rent rules, pay rent by card, split annual rent), once the sitemap issue is fixed so the posts can index.

// 06 · Future Development

New capabilities to build

6.1  Build a RERA rent increase calculator
UAEDevelopmentHigh Impact
What we found

UAE tenants heavily use the official RERA rental index to check the legally permitted rent increase, but Rently has no such tool. In Ahrefs, the head term "rera rent calculator" alone gets about 3,300 searches a month, and the wider low difficulty cluster totals roughly 5,000 a month at keyword difficulty 0 to 4.

Why it matters

This is high intent demand from renters at the exact moment they are evaluating rent, and it is a proven magnet for links and AI citations. It maps perfectly onto a rent focused brand, and the difficulty is low enough to rank quickly.

Recommendation

Build a RERA rent increase calculator that pulls from the official Dubai rental index so it is accurate and trusted. Modelled capture is about plus 550 organic visits a month once it ranks, plus the link and citation value.

6.2  Build a rewards and miles on rent calculator
SingaporeDevelopmentConversion tool
What we found

In Singapore the concept of earning rewards on rent has almost no local search demand yet (the category is emerging, led globally by Bilt), so a calculator here is not a traffic play. The adjacent "pay rent with credit card singapore" cluster is small (~150 a month) but high intent.

Why it matters

Rewards is Rently Singapore's actual differentiator. A calculator that shows "pay 2,500 dollars rent, earn X miles, worth a flight to Bangkok" is a strong sign up driver and future proofs Rently as the one rent specific rewards tool AI cites as the category grows.

Recommendation

Build it as an on site conversion and AEO asset, not for day one traffic, and point it at the rankable "pay rent with credit card singapore" term. Measure it on sign ups and AI citation share.

6.3  Route AI citations into the product and homepage
SingaporeUAEDevelopmentHigh Impact
What we found

AI already cites Rently heavily, but through blog articles. In Singapore, 28 of 35 cited URLs are blog pages. The homepage and product pages, which carry the conversion, are rarely cited.

Why it matters

Brand level AI visibility only becomes revenue when the pages that convert are the ones cited. This is the single highest leverage move in the whole report, because the audience and the citations already exist.

Recommendation

Add Product and Service schema and strong, keyword aligned titles and headings to the homepage and product pages (findings 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, 5.1, 5.3), and add natural internal links from the cited blog articles into the matching product page so blog authority flows toward the pages that sell.

// 07 · What to Prioritise

Sequencing the work by commercial impact

The data and findings point to one theme in both markets: AI already recommends Rently, but the foundation and the money pages are the weak link. Tackle the highest impact, lowest effort work first.

  1. UAE, add the blog to the sitemap. The fastest win in the report. It unlocks the dormant blog that is already carrying Rently's AI lead (4.4, 5.4).
  2. Make the money pages citable and rankable. Fix the hidden and duplicated titles and headings, and give the homepage a transactional keyword, in both markets (4.2, 5.1, 5.3). This is what routes AI citations to the pages that convert.
  3. Complete the structured data layer. Singapore has none that renders; the UAE has it on product pages but not the homepage or blog. Standardise FAQPage, BreadcrumbList, Article, and Product across both sites (4.1, 4.3, 4.5).
  4. Route blog authority into product pages with internal links and product schema, so the existing AI visibility starts converting (6.3).
  5. Reactivate and clean the blogs. Retire or re optimise the ~120 dead Singapore pages, and publish against high intent clusters in the UAE (5.2, 5.4).
  6. Build the calculators. A RERA rent increase calculator in the UAE for traffic (6.1), and a rewards on rent calculator in Singapore for conversion and category ownership (6.2).
  7. Fix mobile speed and semantic structure on both homepages, starting with the 22.5 second Singapore LCP (4.6, 4.7, 4.8).
