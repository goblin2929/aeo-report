Executive Summary

Period in Review: a softer week on demand, but the fundamentals improved

Every area of the Globe scope eased this week (Globe -9.03%, Converter ID -2.74%, Converter EN -9.78%, international blogs -11.06%), but the softness is demand and FX-search seasonality, not ranking. The clearest evidence: Flip Globe clicks fell while its average position improved from 8.5 to 8.2 (non-branded Globe from 8.5 to 7.5). Reading each area:

  • Flip Globe: fewer clicks, better rankings. Clicks -9.03% and impressions -13.65%, yet position improved. More importantly, the priority pages moved up in rank: China recovered from 9.58 to 7.56 (last week's flagged concern), Singapore from 6.87 to 5.01, and the English Globe hub from 15.15 to 11.52. This is an early positive signal consistent with the destination heading customisation now in staging (Pursuit 1).
  • Currency Converter is the engine, easing on FX seasonality. Indonesian converter clicks -2.74% with position roughly flat (6.7 to 6.9); the big pools (ringgit, USD, riyal, yen, won) held their impressions and positions. The English converter is still tiny and weak, but there is a green shoot: the English usd-usa-idr page grew +63.64% clicks and improved to position 8.58. English remains the single biggest untapped ceiling ("usd to idr" at 1.83 million monthly searches).
  • International blogs eased in line with the whole blog. The 124 overseas-transfer blogs fell -11.06%, close to the whole-site blog (-9.13%), so this is a site-wide seasonal dip rather than a scope-specific problem. Positions largely held. The SWIFT-code cluster (bri, bni, seabank, mandiri, bca) remains the standout opportunity: large impression pools at 0.2% to 0.7% CTR.
  • Carry-forward hygiene: the converter hreflang tags between the Indonesian and English versions are still broken (they point to a 404 and a redirect). The fix is restated in Additional Pursuits.

3. Technical hygiene: broken converter hreflang between the ID and EN versions (reminder)

Carried forward from the previous meeting. The Indonesian and English converters use different path slugs (/id/kalkulator-mata-uang/ vs /en/currency-converter/), but the hreflang alternate tags only swap the /id/ and /en/ prefix and keep the wrong path. The result: each language points to a broken URL for the other, so Google cannot pair the two versions. Verified live:

  • On the ID page, the en-US alternate points to /en/kalkulator-mata-uang/... which returns 404.
  • On the EN page, the id-ID and x-default alternates point to /id/currency-converter/... which returns a 301 redirect (hreflang should point to the final 200 URL, not a redirect).

Example page pair: /id/kalkulator-mata-uang/idr-mys-myr and /en/currency-converter/idr-mys-myr.
