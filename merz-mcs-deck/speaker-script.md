# Ultherapy Asia — GEO proposal · speaker script

21 slides · ~25 minutes · Merz (HuiQing Yeo) with M&C Saatchi Performance

**How to use this.** Each slide has one job, written as *Beat*. Say the spoken lines,
then the *Bridge* into the next slide. Do not read the slide — the slide carries the
numbers, you carry the argument. Where a number appears below, it is because you are
saying it out loud; everything else on the slide is there for them to read while you talk.

**The argument in one line:** Ultherapy is known everywhere and is the source almost
nowhere — and the decision now happens inside an answer written by somebody else.

---

## ACT 1 — The ground moved (slides 1–5)

### SLIDE 1 — Cover

*Beat:* Set the frame before any data.

> Ultherapy is not an awareness case. Across these five markets the brand is known,
> discussed, and asked for by name. So this is not a proposal about getting known.
> It is about a narrower question: when someone is deciding, who is the source the
> answer gets built from? Right now, in most of these markets, it isn't Merz.

*Bridge:* "Let me start with what changed underneath all of this."

---

### SLIDE 2 — Zero-click

*Beat:* Name the loss of control. This is the pain the whole deck resolves.

> Search used to hand you the customer. Someone typed a question, saw ten links, and
> chose one — and once they landed on your site, the journey was yours to run.
> That handoff is disappearing. The answer is now assembled and delivered on the
> results page, and the customer never arrives to be persuaded.
> You have not lost the audience. You have lost the part of the journey you controlled.

*Bridge:* "And the reason this is accelerating has nothing to do with aesthetics."

---

### SLIDE 3 — Humans as a rounding error

*Beat:* This is not a trend deck. Two facts, then move.

> Cloudflare sees a large share of the world's web traffic, so when their CFO says
> humans will be a rounding error on the internet, he is describing his own logs.
> In May, machine traffic overtook human traffic on their network for the first time.
> They had forecast that for 2027. It came a year early.
> The practical version for Merz: one person comparing treatments opens five clinic
> sites. An agent doing it for them opens thousands. The reader you are writing for
> has already changed.

*Bridge:* "So let's look at what that reader is actually saying about your category."

---

### SLIDE 4 — Data methodology

*Beat:* Buy credibility before you spend it. Twenty seconds, then move.

> Before I show you anything about Merz — everything after this slide was fetched from
> live engines in the last 48 hours. None of it is assumed, and none of it is a template.
> Six thousand queries across your five markets. Eighty-four real buyer questions, asked
> in each market's own language. Two hundred and fifty-two answers read and scored one by
> one. And ten of your own pages crawled against a competitor's.
> If you disagree with a number later, we can show you the run behind it.

*Bridge:* "So — what does the category look like in those answers?"

---

### SLIDE 5 — The new storefront

*Beat:* Make it visceral and specific. Three live answers, one uncomfortable pattern.

> These are live answers, captured yesterday. Same market, same question — different engine.
> In Taiwan, Google puts Ultraformer first and Ultherapy second, under a generic name.
> In Taiwan, ChatGPT puts Ultherapy PRIME first, as its top pick.
> Same market. Same question. Opposite winner.
> That is what an unmanaged category looks like: the ranking changes with the engine,
> not with the market — because nothing in the answer is anchored to a source you own.
> This is your storefront now. Nobody at Merz has ever signed it off.

*Bridge:* "That's the impression. Here is the measurement behind it."

---

## ACT 2 — You are known, but you are not the source (slides 6–9)

### SLIDE 6 — Decision problem

*Beat:* Introduce the one distinction the entire deck runs on: **named** vs **cited**.

> Two different things get measured here, and the gap between them is the whole story.
> **Named** means the brand appears in the answer. **Cited** means a Merz page was the
> source the answer was built from.
> Across two hundred-odd unbranded answers, Ultherapy is named eight times in ten.
> Merz is the cited source four times in ten. So the brand travels — on somebody else's
> authority.
> And it gets worse the closer you get to the decision. In Taiwan, at the top of the
> funnel, the brand is named in two-thirds of answers and cited in six per cent.
> [*click* — reveal the cited column]
> You do not have an awareness problem. You have a decision problem.

*Bridge:* "At prompt level it's even clearer."

---

### SLIDE 7 — The measured gap

*Beat:* Show the mechanism. Don't read the table — read two rows.

> Look at the first row and the last row.
> "What is Ultherapy" — named 100%, cited 100%. When the buyer already has you in mind,
> the answer confirms you, and it uses your own page to do it.
> "What treatment is best for sagging jowls and jawline" — zero and zero. When they are
> describing a problem instead of a product, you are not in the room.
> So you win the customers who had already decided. Which is the definition of a brand
> that is visible but not persuasive.

*Bridge:* "One reason for that is structural, and it's on your side of the fence."

---

### SLIDE 8 — Thin sites

*Beat:* The owned-media problem, stated without blame.

> Two hundred and seventy-six thousand searches a month across the five markets.
> Merz properties capture two point seven per cent of it.
> In Thailand and Taiwan, no Merz domain ranks at all.
> Galderma and Allergan each run one host. Merz runs four properties plus an empty
> regional domain — so nothing accumulates. Every market starts from zero, forever.

*Bridge:* "And the demand that is going uncaptured is the demand that matters most."

---

### SLIDE 9 — Where the demand is

*Beat:* Close Act 2 by joining the two halves — you lose the unbranded question, and the unbranded question is the market.

> In Hong Kong, 64% of category search never says "Ultherapy". In Australia, 62%.
> In Thailand, half.
> That is the exact question type where you are named but not cited.
> Taiwan is the one inversion — 48% is brand-named — which is why Taiwan is a
> defend-the-brand market and the others are win-the-category markets.
> Put simply: you are strongest where the demand is smallest.

*Bridge:* "So what does winning actually take? It is not what most people assume."

---

## ACT 3 — What winning takes (slides 10–11)

### SLIDE 10 — Three moves

*Beat:* The pivot from pain to plan. This is where you say what we do that others don't.

> Most agencies will answer this with a website redesign. A beautiful site does not
> make you citable — engines quote passages, not pages, and they quote from wherever
> the passage happens to live.
> So there are three moves, and only three.
> One: a site a machine can read. Today there is no H1, canonicals are empty,
> and there is no structured data at all.
> Two: content built as answers, not brochures.
> Three — and this is the one most people skip — influence the third-party sources the
> engines actually cite. PubMed is cited 165 times on Merz's own technology. None of
> those citations point to Merz.
> The first two are yours to own. The third is earned, and it is where the answer
> is decided.
> **What that buys you is not traffic. It is that the patient arrives already persuaded** —
> because a source they trust named you while they were still shortlisting.

*Bridge:* "Let me take the three in order, starting with the one everything else sits on."

---

### SLIDE 11 — Section divider

*Beat:* Breath. One line only.

> First move: a site a machine can read.

## ACT 4 — The proposal (slides 12–16)

### SLIDE 12 — What the machine finds

*Beat:* Diagnosis, side by side with a competitor so it isn't abstract.

> This is your regional domain against a clinic site that competes with you for the
> same answers. Not a global brand — a clinic.
> They have structured data on every page. You have none. They have a heading structure.
> Your homepage has no H1 and no H2s.
> The engine is not choosing them over you on quality. It is choosing them because on
> your pages there is nothing to lift.

*Bridge:* "And when I say nothing to lift, I mean it literally. Here is one page."

---

### SLIDE 13 — Built in 2016

*Beat:* The proof slide. Slow down — this is the one they will remember.

> This is your Hong Kong comparison page. To a person, it is a clean comparison table:
> Ultherapy against surgery, against laser, with depths in millimetres.
> To a machine, this entire page contains one thing: a file called compare-table01a.
> Every number in that table — every depth, every temperature, every recovery time —
> is coloured pixels inside a JPEG built in 2016.
> Zero table elements. Zero rival names as text. The alt text is the filename.
> So when someone asks an engine "Ultherapy or Thermage", your best comparison asset
> cannot participate in the answer. It has been invisible for ten years.
> Google can reach this page. No engine can read a single fact on it.

*Bridge:* "Fixing the page is not enough, though, if the fix lands on four different domains."

---

### SLIDE 14 — One domain

*Beat:* Technical, so keep it short — then answer the migration objection before it is asked.

> Five markets, one destination, each with its own localised content.
> The detail that matters: Singapore and Australia are both in English on one domain.
> Without correct locale signals they compete with each other. Today your two Singapore
> domains target the same queries and declare no relationship at all — so you are
> splitting your own authority.
>
> And on the risk of consolidating — eighty-four thousand searches a month in Taiwan
> and Thailand have no Merz domain ranking at all. That part is unclaimed, so there is
> nothing to lose, and that is where we start. The seven thousand monthly visits that
> do exist get moved deliberately, not dropped.

*Bridge:* "Once there is one destination, the question is what to put on it."

---

### SLIDE 15 — Content architecture

*Beat:* Show that the build order is derived, not invented. This is the credibility slide.

> These clusters are not a content plan someone brainstormed. Each one is a real question
> pattern, scored on whether a Merz page was the source.
> Read the cited column as the running order: the lower the number, the sooner we build it,
> because that is where you are least often the source. Concerns and outcomes — where the buyer describes
> a problem instead of a product — is cited 20% of the time. That is where we start.
> Two worth calling out. Clinical evidence: 165 PubMed citations exist on your own
> technology and none of them resolve to you — that is a page you are entitled to own
> and nobody else can write.
> And authenticity — how to tell a real Ultherapy from a counterfeit machine. That claim
> is only credible from the manufacturer. No clinic can make it. No competitor can take it.

*Bridge:* "That covers what you own. The third move is what you don't."

---

### SLIDE 16 — Off-page citations

*Beat:* Turn the manufacturer constraint into the strategy. This is the differentiation slide — land it.

> Merz is a manufacturer, not a provider. You cannot quote prices and you should not be
> chasing best-clinic lists. That constraint is exactly where the leverage is.
> Here is the difference between this and ordinary digital PR. Most agencies would take a
> standard media list and pitch it. We start from the citations themselves: across your
> answers there are 1,074 third-party citations spread over 271 domains — and we know
> which ones, per market, in language.
> Read the right-hand column. Thirty per cent of them are regulators and journals —
> that is your own MFU-V evidence being cited, and never back to you.
> Twenty-eight per cent are clinics and providers. That is your own distribution network,
> already being quoted while Merz is absent. Those are the people we approach, with
> pre-approved, schema-marked material they publish and attribute back to Merz.
> Their authority, carrying your claims.
> And communities are only four per cent of citations but far more of the conversation —
> in Taiwan, Dcard alone carries 1,600 searches a month attached to this category.

*Bridge:* "So that is what we would build. Here is how it runs."

## ACT 5 — How we work (slides 17–21)

### SLIDE 17 — Section divider

*Beat:* One line.

> Phased against your April 2027 go-live, with three numbers reported monthly.

---

### SLIDE 18 — Roadmap

*Beat:* Sequence logic, and the speed claim.

> This is sequenced by capability, not by market — the architecture gets built once and
> proven in one market before it is repeated. Building five markets in parallel means
> making the same mistake five times.
> Month one is the site and the inputs. Months two and three, content and outreach run
> together. Month three, we re-run the same prompts against the same engines and show
> you movement.
> That timeline is only possible because delivery runs on our own agentic infrastructure —
> research, content, technical, citation tracking. Work that a conventional agency scopes
> across quarters ships here in weeks, and it is senior operators doing it, not a
> delivery layer.

*Bridge:* "And month three is a measurement, not a status update."

---

### SLIDE 19 — Reporting

*Beat:* Contrast with what they are used to receiving.

> Three numbers. Not rankings, not impressions.
> Citation share: of the tracked prompts, how often is Ultherapy named — and how often
> is a Merz page the cited source. Split by market and question type, so you can see
> which layer is moving.
> Earned share of voice: are the third-party sources saying it.
> Organic capture: is the consolidated domain compounding.
> The first baseline already exists — it is the data you have been looking at for the
> last twenty minutes, captured yesterday.

*Bridge:* "Which brings us to what it costs."

---

### SLIDE 20 — Quotation

*Beat:* Present as a choice of scope, not a price. Six-month term.

> Three scopes, six-month term. They differ on markets covered, prompts tracked and
> pages produced.
> One market on its own is five thousand a month. All five markets, same scope, is
> eight thousand one hundred — sixteen hundred per market. The per-market lines discount
> from the third market, so the regional decision is what makes the economics work.
> Accelerate is the recommendation: twice the tracked prompts and twenty more pages a
> month. In a category where the unbranded question is the majority of demand, the number
> of questions you can see is the constraint on how fast you close the gap.

*Bridge:* "There are three things we need to start, and none of them are budget."

---

### SLIDE 21 — What we need

*Beat:* Close by handing them a decision they can make in the room.

> Search Console and Analytics on all four domains — no Merz property has it today,
> and a consolidation cannot be validated blind.
> Crawler access on the regional domain — in our testing, ultherapy-asia.com returned a
> security challenge to crawlers. If that is real, nothing on it can be indexed or cited.
> Your platform team can confirm that in minutes.
> And regulatory sign-off on which claims sit ungated per market. Gated content is
> invisible to every language model — so that decision sets the limit on everything else.
> The first two are quick. The third is the one worth starting this week.
> If we get those three, we can have your baseline and month-one build running before
> the end of September.

## Two questions to expect

**"Isn't this just SEO?"**
> Rankings measure position in a list. We measure whether a Merz page was the source an
> answer was built from — per prompt, per market, in language. Those move differently:
> your Hong Kong comparison page can rank and still be unreadable to every engine, which
> is exactly what is happening.

**"Why not wait until the site is rebuilt in April?"**
> Because the citation network is earned, and it takes months to build. If we start the
> off-page work now, the new site launches into an environment that already cites you.
> If we wait, April is when the clock starts, not when results start.
